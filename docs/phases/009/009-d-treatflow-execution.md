| Link | Identity/knowledge required before treating the connection as meaningful | Forbidden inference |

**Epistemic rule:** unknown-to-actor, not-entitled-to-inspect, pending verification and affirmatively absent are distinct conditions. R cannot truthfully assert an unseen factual change; D cannot silently approve data when the protective basis or mapping is missing. An appropriate result may be *unknown, unavailable, limited, declined or no new action*. This preserves nonparticipant protection without inventing universal omniscience.

## 3. Directional interference and counterfactual removal

- **S→O / S→V:** Making S association compulsory would defeat O low-burden participation; transferring S standing to V authority would defeat independent factual protection. **Neither coupling exists** in C-01–C-03.
- **V→R:** If later known factual branches were flattened to latest-wins, R could misstate suitability. D-01 preserves known as-of qualified standing and allows an honest insufficient conclusion.
- **R→D / D→R:** If R qualification acted as approval, D protection fails; if D approval acted as evidence certification, R scientific purpose fails. D-02 independently guards both.
- **D→V/R/recipient:** If suspension retroactively rewrote delivery or V truth, history would be false. D-03 does not recall or rewrite.
- **S/D→unaffiliated persons:** A protected Party or reporting source is not a universal disclosure-consent proxy. Missing legitimate third-party basis must remain restrictive rather than transferring implied consent.

**Counterfactual remove C-03:** the combined system becomes vulnerable to using responsibility disputes as factual adjudication. **Remove D-01:** later legitimate known version conflicts could be ignored for research suitability. **Remove D-02:** research fitness/permission can masquerade as each other. **Remove D-03:** new prospective scopes may borrow stale approval. These illustrate why current bounded rules matter; they do not create runtime dependencies or automatic triggers.
