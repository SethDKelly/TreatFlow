## 1. Competing observation decompositions

| Alternative | Possible behavioral ownership | Seeds | Strength worth exploring | Doubt / cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| O-A — Arrival diary | One recognizable act of maintaining arrival encounters; monitored time may be contextual supporting information | S-01/04/05/14 | Familiar and quick for a door-answering contributor (P-01) | Silent periods and attention gaps might be too implicit for P-02/P-03 |
| O-B — Watched-period accounting | A coherent observed-interval practice owns both arrival counts and monitored exposure; individual events can be optional detail | S-02/03/04/26 | Makes actual exposure and zero-versus-missing distinction prominent (P-02) | Can demand work during Halloween and make P-01 harder |
| O-C — Bounded observation claim | An observer makes a claim about an interval/event with stated certainty; event/time/group are attributes or alternative claim forms, not separate concepts | S-05/06/10/14 | May encompass live, estimated and retrospective evidence without pretending uniform precision | Could become too generic, overloaded or difficult to understand |
| O-D — Separable observation and exposure behaviors | One candidate owns reports of occurrences/counts; another owns periods actually watched; they may need later coordination | S-01/02/03/06 | Explicitly distinguishes two different human purposes and failure modes | Independence may be artificial; users could reasonably view both as one act |

**Boundary experiment:** in a quiet hour, O-A risks inferring a zero without evidence; O-B records monitored quiet; O-C requires a credible bounded claim; O-D would need reliable coordination. A group of five must not be transformed into five independent events by any alternative. We do **not** decide whether "arrival" is itself a concept; it may be a real-world unit or a property of a recording behavior.

## 2. Participation, identity and provenance decompositions

| Alternative | Possible behavioral ownership | Seeds | Opportunity | Doubt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I-A — Contributor-centered participation | A recognizable participant relationship owns ability to associate, review and correct contributions | S-07/09/11/23 | Familiar continuity and accountability (P-05) | Easily defaults to permanent identifiable accounts and exclusionary friction (P-01/P-04) |
| I-B — Claim-centered provenance | Each bounded observation has understandability and amendability; identity may be minimal, separate or contextual | S-06/10/11/14 | Treats measurement trust as properties of evidence rather than a person's public profile | Correction authority and abuse handling may be hard to justify without contributor continuity |
| I-C — Independent participation and evidentiary origin | A participation behavior handles agency/control; distinct contribution provenance expresses source/quality | S-07/09/10/23 | Can keep personal identity apart from research-readable evidence (P-04/P-05) | May be needless splitting unless both deliver independent end-to-end value |
| I-D — Scoped/ephemeral participation | Time- or study-bounded ability to submit and revisit contributions without assuming enduring public identity | S-09/25/06 | Could lower privacy and operational burden | Recovery, shared sites and meaningful corrections are unresolved |

**Boundary experiment:** two people at one household can report overlapping periods. Neither account uniqueness nor a shared location necessarily establishes unique observations. Source recognition, household/site identity, and public researcher provenance must not silently collapse into the same identifier.

## 3. Correction, withdrawal and challenge decompositions

| Alternative | Possible behavioral ownership | Seeds | Opportunity | Doubt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-A — Correctable observation lifecycle | Reporting itself owns amendment/retraction of the contributor's own claim | S-06/11/12 | Familiar record correction with modest cognitive burden | Is a public release already irreversible? Do operator challenges need distinct authority? |
| C-B — Independent revision/reconciliation | A coherent revision behavior can amend or reconcile previously contributed evidence | S-11/13/28 | Expresses traceability and disputes beyond initial reporting | May be just a workflow fragment of a broader claim behavior |
| C-C — Separate protected withdrawal | Withdrawal of participation or future release is distinguished from correcting the truth of an observation | S-12/23 | Respects privacy choice as different from factual correction (P-04) | Cannot imply retroactive erasure from external downloads |

**Boundary experiment:** changing five visitors to four corrects evidence; withdrawing consent affects future use/disclosure; flagging fabrication can require distinct authority. The alternatives must not conflate these actions or promise deletion from already distributed copies.

## 4. Research sharing, interpretation and safety decompositions

| Alternative | Possible behavioral ownership | Seeds | Opportunity | Doubt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-A — Shareable evidence collection | A coherent release-oriented behavior presents interpretable observations and limitations; privacy conditions may be intrinsic | S-15/16/17 | Minimal surface area supporting independent researchers (P-02/P-03/P-06) | Risk of hiding disclosure and audience protections in an overly broad behavior |
| R-B — Independent protected disclosure and access | One behavior governs whether information can be released; another governs who may use a defined accessible body | S-16/17/19 | Separates safety from consumption and expresses accountable decisions | Could be artificial coupling or heavyweight for a small operator |

| R-D — External analytical work | TreatFlow's behavioral responsibility ends with responsibly interpretable evidence; notebooks, tests and conclusions belong to researchers outside product | S-15/20/21/22 | Keeps the initiative modest, honors a rejectable Poisson hypothesis | May leave nonexpert consumers without needed interpretive guardrails |

**Boundary experiment:** a small region with very few contributors could permit household linkage even if location is labeled "coarse." R-A and R-B need credible protection semantics; R-C may be safer but analytically limiting; R-D needs disclosure guardrails despite external analysis; R-E must not imply a Poisson-confirming dashboard.

## 5. Cross-domain whole-application decompositions (not candidate catalogs)

These are alternative *ways of allocating behavior across the problem*, deliberately imperfect and not exclusive.

- **W-A — Lightweight diary with protected research release:** event-centric contribution, corrections within the same activity, bounded sharing, external analysis. Strength: low friction. Misfit: active zero intervals and provenance could be weak.
- **W-B — Observation study with explicit coverage:** monitored-period contribution, distinct accountable participation, disclosure assessment, external analysis. Strength: meaningful exposure. Misfit: a heavy study workflow could deter participants.
- **W-C — Evidence claims and controlled use:** generalized observation claims with uncertainty, separate correction/challenge, purpose-limited access. Strength: flexible provenance and protection. Misfit: complex concepts and operator burden.
- **W-D — Privacy-first community insight:** coarse/derived activity reports, nonidentifying public comparison, minimal researcher access to fine details. Strength: protects nonparticipants. Misfit: might abandon scientific reproducibility or event-level analysis.
- **W-E — Bounded community research campaign:** seasonal participation and observation scope integrated as a recognizable study, with study-specific claims and carefully governed sharing. Strength: understandable initiative context. Misfit: campaign may be packaging rather than an independent reusable concept.

No winner is picked; the alternatives are not five fixed proposed product architectures. They expose tradeoffs among P-01–P-06.

## 6. Negative concept-boundary controls

The following remain **possible data, policies, coordination, interfaces or external activities**, even when they appear as seeds, rather than being promoted merely by name:

- **Visitor / Group / Arrival / Geographic category:** real-world observations or classifications; S-04 may not be an independent behavior.
- **Timestamp / confidence / count / exposure duration:** measurement characteristics; S-14 could be a property rather than a concept.
- **CSV download / login / dashboard:** potential mechanisms, not purposes or self-justifying concepts.
- **Poisson calculation:** a scientific method, not necessarily a full user-facing concept; S-21 may belong to external analytics.
- **Privacy suppression rule:** could be an invariant or policy of sharing rather than a distinct behavioral concept.
- **Linking observer and observation:** may be a future synchronization rather than an independent concept.

These are critical questions for [002-D concept-likeness review](002-a-treatflow-execution.md), not definitive rejection decisions today.
