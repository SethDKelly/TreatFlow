Treat internal operational needs as necessary context, not an invented team structure.

## Need and burden inquiry

**Contributors:** The meaningful opportunity is to turn brief household experience into understandable shared evidence without imposing distracting work. A Halloween observer may be interrupted, record late, or conflate individuals with group events. Distinct need candidates are tolerable reporting effort, honest treatment of imprecision, and a way for inaccurate contributions not to silently corrupt analysis. The existence or preferred solution for these needs is unvalidated.

**Analysts:** Their need is not simply possession of a large file. An interpretable series must distinguish observed exposure from missing intervals, preserve what was counted, and make uncertainty/selection effects knowable. A useful result may be rejection of a homogeneous Poisson hypothesis, a time-varying model, or insufficient evidence. We do not infer that professional researchers will participate.

**Household members and observed visitors:** They may not have opted into a reporting system, and have little reason to accept public exposure of a residence's activity or identification of individual children. Their plausible protective needs constrain how benefits to analysts can be pursued. No personal visitor observations or consent practices are designed here.

**Operators:** A small volunteer project requires proportionate safety, correction, quality and release responsibilities. Excessive review and incident handling could make the initiative unsustainable; no staffing, hosting budget or expected participation numbers are known.

**Community:** Trustworthy contextualized insights may be useful, but apparent geographic rankings or scientifically certain claims from a convenience sample could mislead; public demand is unverified.

## Evidence limitations and contrasting possibilities

Some people may happily record individual arrivals live; others may only remember totals later; others may not want an account at all. More structure could improve validity for some and suppress participation for others. Public raw access might aid replication yet amplify linkage risks. Reported event counts might reflect visitors, groups, or bin totals, and cannot be silently treated as exchangeable. This phase records these as competing plausible conditions, not evidence that one participant preference dominates.

No scientific claim is made that an actual sample is Poisson, geographically representative, or accurately timestamped. No jurisdiction-specific privacy/consent obligation is asserted without further qualification.
