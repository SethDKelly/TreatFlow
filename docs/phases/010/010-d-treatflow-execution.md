## 1. Representative success and deliberately inaccessible success

**S04 — protected nonavailability.** A researcher or proposer seeks event-level Halloween time and geographic material about one household. D.Propose records specific Material, Audience and purpose but creates no exposure. D.Assess finds the protection basis for household residents, visiting children and neighboring individuals insufficient or unresolved. D.Decide must **not approve** in the absence of defensible policy and evidence. A legitimately restricted or declined decision can be inspected only within entitled scope without disclosing private reasons or material. **PASS at conceptual level**: protective withholding is an intelligible outcome, independently of research fitness.

**S05 — conditional research provision with disclosure.** Only in an explicitly hypothetical context where independent R fitness, protection criteria, legitimate authority, correct Material/Audience correspondence, conditions and eligible distribution **are already justified**, D-02 permits scoped R.Provide and D.MakeAvailable, preserving necessary caveats and actual ExposureRecord. **Not positively validated for real TreatFlow:** those prerequisites do not currently exist. S05 is **CONDITIONAL ONLY / REAL RELEASE BLOCKED**, not a demonstrated safe path.

## 3. Protected authority and affected-party asymmetry

The actors who **provide observations**, **claim responsibility**, **amend evidence**, **request research**, **evaluate disclosure** and **receive material** have distinct conceptual powers. No private standing or household association gives general consent for an affected visitor, child, neighbor or co-resident. A decision about material scope must consider their potential exposure **without requiring them to become direct app users**.

Where protective evidence, accountable assessor, guardian/consent basis or the consequences of data combinations are unknown, D.Assess must remain insufficient/unresolved and D.Decide cannot affirmatively authorize an unsafe release. The system may have an honest, useful **nonavailability** outcome even when research demand is high.

No concrete authorization hierarchy, guardian verification, age restriction, privacy threshold or retention/deletion promise is created by this phase. These are **explicit design/policy dependencies** to resolve before any operational collection or release.

## 4. Irreversibility, historical recipients and prospective restrictions

1. **Propose ≠ Decide ≠ MakeAvailable.** Proposal and protected assessment do not imply exposure.
2. **Scientific fitness ≠ release legitimacy.** Even useful evidence may remain withheld.
3. **Prospective change:** materially changed evidence, time precision, transformation, audience or purpose requires appropriately authorized scope reconsideration before *future* availability.
4. **Recorded past exposure is not revocable history.** D.SuspendFuture stops later qualified release under its decision; it cannot compel deletion from recipients' possession or unwind downstream linkage.
5. **Withdrawal is not one universal action.** S.Relinquish, V.Amend, D.SuspendFuture, prospective consent decisions and historical copy possession must remain distinct.
6. **Safety must consider compounding information:** repeated and cross-dataset releases may create linkability absent from an individual record; approval based only on a single obvious attribute is not justified by the design.
