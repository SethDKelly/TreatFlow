## 1. External source record and applicability discipline

| Source | Grounded methodological contribution | TreatFlow-specific inference / limitation |
|---|---|---|
| [AAPOR, *Best Practices for Survey Research*](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/) | Nonprobability/opt-in samples require explicit transparency and special inferential caution | Volunteer participants cannot be assumed representative of all neighborhoods; descriptive claims tied to a recruited/participating cohort may be narrower, but no sample quality established |
| [AAPOR Task Force, *Non-Probability Sampling* (2013)](https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/NPS_TF_Report_Final_7_revised_FNL_6_22_13-2.pdf) | Wider-population inference depends on a defensible theoretical basis and clearly articulated assumptions | Avoid unsupported population rates, universal interarrival distribution, and confident city-level model claims; not a finding that every voluntary descriptive analysis is worthless |
| [NIST SP 800-188, *De-Identifying Government Datasets* (2023)](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/188/final) | Disclosure risk must be weighed with meaningful analysis; alternative publication/protected-access models and context-specific risk evaluation matter | Aggregation, pseudonym removal, or coarse geography alone cannot be treated as sufficient evidence of TreatFlow household/visitor protection; this guidance is not a TreatFlow-specific privacy assessment |
| [NIST IR 8053, *De-Identification of Personal Information*](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8053/final) | Removing direct identifiers may still leave reidentification concerns | Repeat releases, fine timing and context remain important; no verified numeric disclosure threshold or policy granted |

**Evidence levels:** these publications are independent **general methodological guidance**, not empirical verification of TreatFlow, not legal advice, and not proof a proposed method meets its own requirements.

## 2. Focused research-purpose alternatives and discriminating test

**K-R1 — bounded descriptive evidence (best candidate for further evaluation).** Report group encounters *as reported* within expressly asserted watched durations and known precision limits, for a clearly defined voluntary observer cohort. Useful questions may include when these contributors reported more/fewer encounters, with event unit, exposure and missingness supplied. **Do not infer broader population representativeness.** Required evidence: examples of report/coverage completeness and volunteer burden, meaningful exposure accounting, sample stability and limits, a non-sensitive research demonstration of informative output. This is a **plausible product direction**, not an accepted scope change.

**K-R2 — narrow model-checking research.** With correctly scoped, sufficiently resolved group-arrival event data, one may investigate whether a defined Poisson model is supported or contradicted *within a specifically qualified observational setting*. Required evidence: coherent watched exposure, event independence/heterogeneity caveats, justified statistical methods and protection of necessary timing detail. The specific positive possibility remains **unproven**.

**K-R3 — legitimate protected research access without public release.** NIST's guidance makes alternative release/access models relevant to future policy consideration. A legitimately entitled researcher receiving an appropriately restricted qualified material scope **might** provide more utility than an unrestricted public download. But this is only an **option to evaluate**, not a newly adopted product variant, access architecture or authorization. Nonparticipant household/minor protections, decision-maker legitimacy, historical recipients and cumulative risk must still be justified.

**K-R4 — suitability-only outcome.** R can truthfully decline unsuitable evidence; this remains a necessary useful outcome but must not stand in for all positive observational/research value.

**Competing explanation check:** seasonal trends may derive from observed coverage changes, inconsistent group/person units, participation selection, reporting gaps or mixed household contexts rather than underlying traffic patterns. Without counterevidence, no stronger conclusion is justified.

## 4. Canonical purpose and scope reentry adjudication

**Decision: NO IMMEDIATE REOPEN of Phase 001/006 on current evidence.** The original purpose promises interpretable evidence that others can explore responsibly, **not** universal public raw release or proof of Poisson arrivals. K-R1 is consistent with the ordinary reading of P-03 and the adopted Research Provision/Controlled Disclosure independent roles. The newly consulted sources provide an external rationale for testing the narrower inquiry. They neither show the existing design impossible nor justify rewriting it to a narrower scope as an exit shortcut.

**Reopen Phase 001/006 if** appropriately gathered external evidence shows responsible shared inquiry cannot provide positive value under the legitimate safeguards, or if a new, user-authorized outcome redefines “others can explore” to a substantially different audience/use. A changed access model with altered material/authority would also require review in the proper concept, composition and mapping owners. Avoid a competing Phase 010 canonical purpose.
